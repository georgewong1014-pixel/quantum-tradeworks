What this map cannot tell you

Named, because a map is the most persuasive thing this product draws.

  • A scheme’s position is a neighbourhood drawn round OpenStreetMap’s evidence for its name — never a building, a lot, a boundary or a title. Confirm any property against its title and the Land and Survey Department.
  • No price, rent or range is drawn on the map. NAPIC’s observed ranges are in the list: a range observed across its sample for one half-year (H1 2025), not the scheme’s price, not the latest sale, and never a midpoint.
  • A scheme not placed is not missing from the market: OpenStreetMap does not name it, or names it only in part. An empty block on the map is not empty land — many suburbs are not yet mapped.
  • Heights are drawn only where OpenStreetMap records one (78 buildings); a flat building is one with no recorded height, not a single-storey one.
  • No flood, zoning or title status is held here, and nothing on the map ranks, scores or recommends a place.

Financial figures come from two different places and are labelled on every page: statements filed with the US SEC, which are audited and real, and illustrative figures for the Malaysian companies and any other company marked illustrative, which are synthetic and created for interface demonstration. No market-data licence is in place for either exchange. A filed company therefore carries no price at all, and every measure that needs one — market capitalisation, multiples, yield, difference to model estimate — reads as unavailable rather than estimated. Nothing here may be used for an investment decision. A production deployment would require licensed market data for both markets and, in Malaysia, written legal classification of each surface under the Capital Markets and Services Act before launch.
