Each purchase route, for each kind of property

What the property calculator needs from you, what it returns and what it will not assume — for a subsale, an auction and a new development, on a residential unit, a commercial unit and land. Every figure is arithmetic on your own inputs, your registers or the fee rulebook. None is a market figure, and nothing is ranked.

Each row is checked against answers worked by hand from these rules, never read back from the calculator.

When a figure is missing

A missing figure is never shown as nought or left blank. It reads as one of these, with its reason.

Unavailable
An input it needs is not entered. The page names the input.
Not final
An auction figure while any of the six checks is open.
Not for this route
A figure of another purchase route.
Not for this asset
A quantity the property does not have — land’s rent, yield and debt-service cover — or a route not written for it.
Not yet checked for commercial property (or land)
A fee line whose rule differs by class. It carries no amount, and the cash required is a partial total that names it.

The routes, one by one

  • Subsale · Residential

    The default, and the route of any deal saved without one.

    You enter
    The price, the down payment, the loan’s rate and tenure, the rent, and the running costs — the sample’s, marked Illustrative, until you change them.
    Optional
    The asking price; comparables named from your register; a tenancy and its rent; condition and age; charges the sale passes to you; a target for the monthly position or the net yield; a valuation; a booking deposit already paid; mortgage protection, left out unless you include it or enter a quote.
    It returns
    The cash required, every fee line with its kind; the monthly position, before tax on the rent; the net yield; the break-even rent and occupancy; the value less the loan at the sale; the rate of return; the gap between the asking price and your comparables; and the highest price that meets your target.
    When it is missing
    With no tenure the loan has no schedule, so the instalment, the reserve and every figure resting on them are Unavailable. With no comparables named, or no asking price, there is no price gap.

  • Subsale · Commercial

    A shop, an office or any other commercial subtype.

    You enter
    As a residential subsale, and the tenancy: tenanted, vacant or not known — for a tenanted unit, its contract rent and lease expiry.
    Optional
    The asking rent; achieved rents named from your register; fit-out for a re-let; rent-free months still to run; a service charge the tenant pays you; escalation, deposit, tenant, frontage, floor and parking, recorded without a figure.
    It returns
    The net yield at the contract rent and at your model rent; the contract rent against the achieved rents you named — highest, lowest and median; the four rents kept apart, never blended; the cash over the rest of the lease, its effective yield and average monthly position; and the reserve and effective yield if the unit stands empty 3, 6, 12 or 18 months from the expiry.
    When it is missing
    A vacant unit has no contract-rent figures. Without the contract rent or the expiry, the rest of the lease is Unavailable and names the one missing.
    Fee lines
    Not yet checked for commercial property, so not charged: Loan legal fees; Searches and the firm’s disbursements; Utility and management deposits; Mortgage protection (MRTA/MLTA) (fee rulebook 1.5.0). Each line, and why

  • Auction · Residential or commercial

    The same model for both.

    You enter
    The winning bid you expect. For the true discount, a comparable — typed with its source and date, or named from your register. For the deposit at risk, the deposit’s per cent, what it is a share of (the reserve or the bid) and the days to pay the balance: never assumed.
    Optional
    The reserve price; arrears passed to you — maintenance and sinking fund, quit rent, assessment, utilities, each only if entered; repairs; the cost and months of getting possession; your lawyer’s quote; a financing buffer; holding months; the six checks; and a bid ceiling, as an all-in cost of your own or a margin under your comparables’ median.
    It returns
    The steps from your comparables’ median to the bid and on to the effective acquisition cost; the true discount — (your comparables’ median − effective cost) ÷ that median; the deposit at risk, with the balance and the cash beyond the loan; and the highest whole-ringgit bid whose effective cost stays at or under your ceiling.
    When it is missing
    Every auction figure reads Not final while any of the six checks is open. A cost not entered is “not entered, not counted”, and named beside the effective cost and the ceiling. A margin ceiling with no comparables’ median is Unavailable.
    Fee lines
    Not yet checked for commercial property, so not charged: Loan legal fees; Searches and the firm’s disbursements; Utility and management deposits; Mortgage protection (MRTA/MLTA) (fee rulebook 1.5.0). Each line, and why
    Legal fees
    Not decided by the Rules: rule 1’s full scale on the bid stands until you enter your lawyer’s quote, which takes its place. Why, rule by rule

  • New development · Residential

    You enter
    The SPA price. For the premium, a completed comparable. For the interest during construction: your schedule of progressive drawdown — stages adding to 100%, each with its month after signing — the SPA month, the month of vacant possession and a loan.
    Optional
    Developer rebates and incentives, taken off the cash required; your lawyer’s quote for the purchase legal fees, in place of the fee rulebook’s; the rent and occupancy at completion; furnishing; a completion delay in whole months.
    It returns
    The premium over the comparable; the interest during construction, a line of the cash required; the monthly position from vacant possession; with the SPA month and the month of vacant possession entered, the rate of return timed from vacant possession — the cash required leaves at signing, no rent and no instalment come before the keys, the holding period you enter counts from them, the sale falls that many years after them at the price grown from signing, and its gains tax counts from the SPA date; exit values three, five and ten years after it — modelled on your growth assumption, not a forecast; the rent and the yearly growth that would justify the premium; and what a delay adds.
    When it is missing
    The interest during construction is Unavailable until the schedule, its months and both dates are entered. Without both months the rate of return and the holding period count from signing, as though the property were let at once, and the calculator says so. Sarawak’s prescribed stage percentages are offered as a template, never applied for you. Without a comparable there is no premium and no exit value.
    Legal fees
    Not decided by the Rules: rule 1’s full scale on the SPA price stands until you enter your lawyer’s quote, which takes its place. Why, rule by rule
    What changed
    Until 13 October 2026 the calculator’s rate of return and its holding period counted from the purchase, as though the property were let on signing. They now count from vacant possession once both months are entered.

  • New development · Commercial

    You enter
    As a residential new development, on your own schedule.
    It returns
    As a residential new development, the rate of return timed from vacant possession the same way.
    Not for this asset
    Sarawak’s prescribed stages and its 18-month defect liability are those of the sale and purchase forms for housing accommodation (the 2014 Regulations). They are not offered or stated for a commercial unit: its SPA sets its own stages and terms — enter yours.
    Fee lines
    Not yet checked for commercial property, so not charged: Loan legal fees; Searches and the firm’s disbursements; Utility and management deposits; Mortgage protection (MRTA/MLTA) (fee rulebook 1.5.0). Each line, and why
    Legal fees
    Not decided by the Rules: rule 1’s full scale on the SPA price stands until you enter your lawyer’s quote, which takes its place. Why, rule by rule

  • Subsale or auction · Land

    A bare parcel.

    You enter
    As the route asks, less everything a tenancy would need: land has no rent, vacancy, net operating income, yield, debt-service cover, break-even rent or empty months, and each reads “not a quantity this asset has”.
    It returns
    The monthly position — the instalment and the costs a parcel does carry: assessment, quit rent and insurance; the price gap against land sales, by land area. A yield target is not applicable.
    Not for this asset
    A bare parcel has no strata body, so there are no maintenance or sinking-fund arrears to pass on.
    Fee lines
    Not yet checked for land, so not charged: Valuation fee; Searches and the firm’s disbursements; Utility and management deposits; Mortgage protection (MRTA/MLTA) (fee rulebook 1.5.0). Each line, and why

  • New development · Land

    It returns
    Nothing of the route runs: no premium, no interest during construction, no rebate and no exit values. The cash required is the subsale’s, and the page says Not for this asset.
    Not for this asset
    The developer premium model prices a building bought off plan — stages of construction, vacant possession, a completed unit to set the premium against. A bare parcel has none of these, so it is not run for land.

A quotation you enter — your lawyer’s, a mortgage protection premium — stands for any kind of property. No commercial or land rate is made up. Open the calculator
