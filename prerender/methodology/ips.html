One deviation from the IPS as written

Section 8 requires every decision to state Buy, Watch/Hold or Reject, and section 10 calls the output a recommendation. This product publishes neither, and the reason is not stylistic.

It is research only. It holds no licence to advise and makes no personal recommendation, and the disclosure on every page rests on that. The one place it takes figures about you is the property calculator’s optional loan-readiness check: the income, debts and credit record you choose to enter stay in this browser and are used only to judge whether a loan is within reach — a diagnostic, not a suitability assessment. The substance of section 8 is kept in full; only the verb changes.

The ten principles

IPS §2. Malaysia-first, cross-asset, evidence-based.

  1. Capital preservation before return maximisationAvoid permanent impairment before pursuing upside.
  2. Evidence before narrativeClaims must rest on financial statements, filings, market data, verified transactions or authoritative public data.
  3. Value is future economics, not a previous high priceA large historical decline does not by itself create a bargain.
  4. Cash flow is net of realistic costsHeadline yield and gross profit are insufficient.
  5. Real demand must existDurable customer demand and business economics for equities; occupier, tenant and resale demand for property.
  6. Entry price mattersA good asset can be a poor investment bought without a margin of safety.
  7. Liquidity and exit are analysed before entryWho the future buyer may be, and what would trigger a sale.
  8. Risk is managed by rejection, sizing, tranches and diversificationNot by confidence.
  9. Every position requires an invalidation conditionIf the thesis breaks, it is reassessed.
  10. All investments compete for capitalProperty is compared with equities and cash on risk-adjusted, after-cost returns.

An asset meets this methodology only when verified quality and real demand, conservative net economics, a sufficient margin of safety, manageable downside and a credible exit all exist at the same time.

IPS §11 states this as an instruction. It is published here as the condition it describes, for the same reason section 8 is.

The eight gates

IPS §3. Every asset, same order. A high score does not carry an asset past a failing gate.

#GateThe question it asks
1Eligibility and investabilityIs the asset lawful, understandable, financeable and practically investable?
2Capital requirementHow much cash, financing capacity and liquidity buffer does it need?
3Demand and qualityWhat produces durable demand, revenue, rent or resale interest?
4Return engineWhat actually generates the return?
5Net economicsWhat is left after financing, tax, vacancy, fees, maintenance and dilution?
6Valuation and margin of safetyIs the entry price sufficiently below a conservatively estimated value?
7Execution planEntry level, tranches, position size, risk limit and monitoring conditions?
8Exit and liquidityWho may buy later, how liquid is the market, and what triggers a change?

The property screening order

IPS §6.1. Fixed, and the order is the control.

Financeability is answered first so that an attractive price cannot hide an asset a bank will not lend against, and yield comes fourth so that nobody reaches it before establishing who would rent the place.

  1. Loanability and bank valuationAn asset a bank will not lend against at the price agreed is not cheap; it is unfinanceable. This is first because everything below is void if it fails.
  2. Cash budget and financing capacityWhat must actually be found in cash, and whether the borrower can carry it.
  3. Real location demandWho has a recurring reason to occupy or buy here.
  4. Rental yieldGross first, so the gap to net is visible rather than assumed away.
  5. Net cash flowAfter every cost that actually recurs.
  6. Buy-in discount and margin of safetyHow far below a conservative value the entry price sits.
  7. Renovation or value-add feasibilityOnly value-add if the rent or resale benefit exceeds its full cost and risk.
  8. Exit strategy and subsale liquidityWho buys it next, and how long that takes.

Auto-reject conditions

IPS §6.9. Under the IPS any one of these refuses regardless of every other figure. Beside each is what this engine actually does with it.

  • Severe or persistent negative cash flowEnforced — the net-economics gate refuses a negative monthly position.
  • Weak real tenant and owner-occupier demandEnforced once demand has been examined — the demand gate refuses when no operating source of demand is recorded, and stays open while nothing has been recorded at all.
  • Severe oversupply or excessive substitutabilityNot tested. The engine holds no supply or unsold-stock data; your checklist answer on nearby supply is raised as a finding, not refused on.
  • Poor management or deteriorating common propertyNot tested. No management or common-property evidence is modelled.
  • Material legal, title or compliance complexityEnforced for the title class — the eligibility gate refuses a restricted class. Other legal or compliance complexity is not tested.
  • Insufficient bank valuation or weak loanabilityEnforced — the margin gate refuses a bank valuation below the price. With no valuation entered the gate stays open.
  • No credible exit marketNot refused on. The exit gate counts sourced transactions you recorded; with none it stays open, because an absence of records is not evidence of no market.
  • Dependence on unsupported appreciation assumptionsEnforced — the return-engine gate refuses a deal with no positive net rent and no recorded value-add.
  • A promotional yield that cannot be independently verifiedNot refused on. An unverified rent keeps the underwriting grade at U, but the IPS assessment does not refuse on it.

Where a condition is enforced, a refusal produces no assessment at all rather than a lower one — a deduction can be outweighed by a good number somewhere else, and that is exactly what must not happen here. Where it is not yet tested, it is said so beside it rather than implied.

What is not implemented

Stated because a published methodology that overstates its own coverage is worse than none.

  • IPS §4 tier 3 — no market-data provider is licensed to this product. NAPIC transaction data can be sourced and analysed; record-level republication is licence-pending, and the data-sources page lists the seven permissions still to be confirmed.
  • IPS §5 — the equity engine answers the eight gates only for property so far. Equities and the cash wheel still use their own scorecards.
  • IPS §7 — cross-asset comparison of after-cost risk-adjusted forward return is not built.
  • IPS §9 — portfolio-level correlation, concentration and stress testing across positions is not built.
  • IPS §6.2 — the mortgage stress test here uses three percentage points, not the one point the IPS specifies. The more conservative figure was already in place and has been kept; this is a deliberate departure and not an oversight.
